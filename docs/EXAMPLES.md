# Real literary and documentary examples (found, not invented)
Nothing here was written by me. Entries say where each was found; passages themselves still have to be copied from the original publications. Banat and Moldavian only so far.

## Banat
- Victor Vlad Delamarina: poetry in Banat grai; dialect poetry is said to begin with him (first published 13 March 1903, per diacronia.ro A1874, M. M. Deleanu "Un secol de poezie dialectală cultă în Banat", Dacoromania serie nouă III–IV, 1998–1999, pp. 27–32). Delamarina's poems are cited for the Lugoj area by At. M. Marienescu, "Dialectul român bănățean", Lugoj, 1902 (as cited by Deleanu).
- George Gârda and other continuers of Delamarina; Marius Munteanu; Ion Viorel Boldureanu (creator of the radio character Cola lu Tutubă, Radio Timișoara; also dialect prose/drama); Ionel Iacob Bencei; Virgil Șchiopescu; Pătru Chira; Ion Curea (dialect poems in "Semenicul"); Vasile Simulescu (poetry in Banat grai after 1989, from Seceani); Nichifor Mihuța (Caransebeș area). Sources: diacronia.ro A1874, dacoromania.inst-puscariu.ro 1998-1999_3.pdf, diacronia.ro V1882, codanec.eu "Literature in the language of Banat grai".
- Anthologies: Aurel Turcuș (ed.), "Poezie în grai bănățean", vol. I, Ed. Orizonturi Universitare, Timișoara, 2009, ISBN 978-973-638-414-1 (via en.wikipedia "Victor Vlad Delamarina"); collection "Grai bănățan", Ed. Dacia Europa Nova, Lugoj, since 1998; festival "Tata Oancea", Bocșa.
- Scholarship on this literature: Gabriel Țepelea, "Literatura în grai bănățean" (doctoral thesis) and "Plugarii condeieri din Banat. Literatura în grai bănățean", Ed. Marineasa, Timișoara, 2005; Cristina Sicoe, "Împrumuturi de origine sârbă în literatura dialectală bănățeană", 2015 (izdanja.filfak.ni.ac.rs).
- Field transcription: the en.wikipedia Banat page includes an IPA transcription of a spoken passage ("Întâmplarea a fost așa...", with standard Romanian version); usable as a test case, with attribution (CC BY-SA).

## Moldavian
- Ion Creangă, "Amintiri din copilărie" (from the Neamț area). Dialect forms documented by Căprioară (2002, diacronia A3599): chilos, chilit, chiuă, să chirotească, pochii, hi (f→h), givorniță, ghiavole/ghiavoli and ghibăcia (put in the mouths of Smaranda and the narrator Nică), mă spariu, o mâniet, înfuriet, vedé, vré. Căprioară and Iordan (preface to Opere, Minerva 1970) caution that Creangă is a literary writer, not a regional transcriber.
- Further studies of Creangă's language found: diacronia.ro A29172 (lexical variation), A10184 (Russian loanwords), V1891 (compares Creangă's Amintiri with Marin Sorescu's La Lilieci).
- Field transcription: the en.wikipedia Moldavian page includes an IPA transcription of a spoken passage (the "two cows" story, with standard version); usable as a test case. Note the page copy I read is from 2016.
- Adevărul.ro, Moldavian expressions chosen by ethnographer Dan Ravaru (stuchit la furcă etc.): lexical, for later.

## Maramureș
- Tache Papahagi, "Graiul și folklorul Maramureșului", Academia Română, București, 1925 (series "Din viața poporului român", XXXIII): dialect texts and glossary; reviewed by N. Drăganu (BCU Cluj copy, dspace.bcucluj.ro). Papahagi's earlier collections "Balade, Colinde și Bocete din Maramureș" (vol. I) and "Cântece poporane din Maramureș" (vol. II), București, 1924, are cited in the same review.
- The en.wikipedia "Maramureș dialect" article includes an IPA transcription of a spoken passage ("Se roagă lui Dumnezeu...") with the standard version; usable as a test case (CC BY-SA).

## Bessarabian and Bukovinian
- No literary dialect writers found yet.
- Documentary texts: "Graiurile românești de la est de Nistru în procesul de consolidare a statului Republica Moldova (Texte dialectale, glosar, studii)" (Institutul de Filologie Română "B. P. Hasdeu", Chișinău; described at ieg.asm.md); Atlasul lingvistic moldovenesc, vol. I–II (1968–73) and ALRR Basarabia (Pavel et al., 1993–2003) with dialect texts; Emil Petrovici, "Texte dialectale" (supplement to ALR II, 1943) (as cited in TUR-77); Turculeț 2002 monograph on the Câmpulung area includes written samples by local speakers (mentioned in the diacronia review R36).

## Crișean / Ardelenesc
- No literary writers in Crișean or Ardelenesc dialect found in this round (only Banat has an established cult dialect literature per Deleanu/Țepelea).
- Documentary: the Crișana dialect page includes an IPA transcription of a spoken passage (dog and sheep story) with standard text; NALR-Crișana volumes I–II (Noul atlas lingvistic român pe regiuni, Crișana) with texts; Petrovici, "Graiul românesc de pe Crișuri și Someș", Transilvania 72 (1941), 551–558 (cited by Farcaș, lingv.ro 2023); Guia, "Dialectologie română. Studii și corpus de texte", Iași, Vasiliana '98, 2014 (cited in NE-TRA, includes a corpus of dialect texts); Rus (2019–) ongoing recorded texts from Valea Bârgăului (55 speakers; unpublished at the time of the article).

## Oltenian
- Marin Sorescu, "La Lilieci" (poetry cycle, ~400 pp.): a diacronia.ro study (V1891, formerly titled "La lilieci sau Marin Sorescu în grai oltenesc") counts Oltenian dialect components at 61.35% in a 10% sample of the text; it compares Creangă's Amintiri with it.
- Dialect text collections: Texte dialectale. Oltenia, ed. B. Cazacu (Cohuț, Ghiculete, Mărdărescu, Șuteu, Vulpe), București, 1967 (one sample from Verbița); Glosar dialectal. Oltenia (Cazacu et al., 1967); Dorina Bărbuț, Dicționar de grai oltenesc, Craiova, 1980.

## Muntenian
- No literary dialect found. Texte dialectale. Muntenia I (Ghiculete, Lăzărescu, Marin, Marinescu, Pană, Vulpe, ed. Cazacu), București, 1973 (sample Nucșoara) and Muntenia III (Bratu et al., 1987; sample Răscăieți); Texte dialectale și glosar. Dobrogea (Lăzărescu et al., 1987); Neagoe & Mărgărit, Graiuri dacoromâne din nordul Bulgariei. Texte dialectale (2006). Source: lingv.ro "Harta sonoră a graiurilor" (2014).
- The en.wikipedia Wallachian page includes an IPA transcription of a spoken passage (midwife scene).

## Timok
- Not literature in the cult-dialect sense: OEAW lists a Vlach Gospel translation, anthologies of Vlach fairy tales, nursery rhymes and children's writing (2000s–2010s), Vlach textbooks "Vuorba și kultura Vlaha" (2013), and Paun Es Durlić's "Language of the Vlach Bread" (Balkankult, 2011). The OEAW site hosts an audio/video collection of Timok Vlach speech (laments, oral traditions, village biographies). Also the Atlas lingvistic al Regiunii Valea Timocului (Neiescu, Beltechi, Mocanu, Reșița, 2006).

## Added to Banat
- Mărgărit 2021 (diacronia A32495) discusses Uzdin (Vojvodina) speech as reflected in dialect literature; it cites a three-act dialect play by Todor Crețu-Toșa from Uzdin, included in "Antologia literaturii dialectale bănățene (poezie, proză, teatru) 1881–2011".
