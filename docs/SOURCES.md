# Sources and verification status
Rule statuses in `js/rules.js`: **sourced** (backed by a page I actually read, keys below) or **unverified** (not checked yet).

## Keys
- **EN-BAN**: en.wikipedia.org "Banat Romanian dialect" (read via a mirror of the full article). Cites Ursan 2008, Bădescu (Craiova course), Caragiu Marioțeanu 1975, Micle 2018.
- **EN-MOL**: en.wikipedia.org "Moldavian dialect". CAUTION: the only full copy I could read is a mirror of the **8 Aug 2016** revision, so it may be older than the live page. Cites Tratat de dialectologie românească (1984), ALR (1938-42), Caragiu Marioțeanu 1975.
- **DIA-BAN**: diacronia.ro/indexing/details/A12911 (study of south-west Banat speech): confirms palatalized dentals in the notation ćínăr, lápćě, fráćě, frúnćě, viđ¢m, prínđe and lists local features (e.g. synaeresis, consonantization of i in capt "caut").
- **CAP-02**: Cosmin Căprioară, "Trăsături dialectale în opera lui Ion Creangă", Analele Univ. Ovidius, XIII, 2002 (diacronia.ro A3599). Confirms for northern Moldavia: ia>ie, final -ea>-é, ea>a after hard consonants, labial palatalization (chilos, chiuă, hi, ghilit), invariable possessive a, auxiliary o; says dental palatalization is absent in Moldavian proper and appears in Creangă only via his mother's Transylvanian-influenced speech; m is generally NOT palatalized.
- **TOT**: en.wikipedia.org "Totoiana" (pahar→harupa, masă→săma, vaca→cava; "not a dialect").

## Corrections to my first draft
- Banat: my first draft said ce/ci→țe/ți and z→dz before e/i. The sources do NOT support that; Banat actually has dental palatalization (t,d→ć,đ), ce/ci→șʲ, and dz only in a few words. Removed.
- Crișean / Ardelenesc: my z→dz rule is contradicted by EN-MOL, which says z (not dz) is the outcome in the Criș dialect and standard Romanian. Removed. EN-MOL lists dz for Maramureș, Banat, Moldavian, Aromanian; Maramureș not yet researched.

## Added in round 2 (Bessarabian, Bukovinian, Maramureș)
- **EN-MAR**: en.wikipedia.org "Maramureș dialect" (full article; cites Caragiu Marioțeanu 1975 pp. 159, 171; Bădescu; Ursan 2008). Includes an IPA field transcription ("Se roagă lui Dumnezeu...").
- **FEL**: Nicolae Felecan, Maramureș study on diacronia.ro V704 (cites Weigand 1908: sier = fier, zin = vin; Tratat: young speakers say sin, old say fin). Also Felecan, "Ion Coteanu și graiul maramureșean", diacronia V136 (Coteanu: Maramureș is a "grai" of a transition zone).
- **EN-BUK**: en.wikipedia.org "Bukovinian Romanian dialect": NO phonetic section, only history, five sub-dialects (Dornean, Câmpulungean, Rădăuțean, Southeast "africatizantă", Eastern), faster tempo and falling unstressed syllables. This is why I used Turculeț.
- **TUR-77**: Adrian Turculeț, "Structura dialectală a graiurilor românești din Bucovina", Anuar de lingvistică și istorie literară 26 (1977–78) 97–120, diacronia.ro A1448. The PDF text is OCR-damaged: some example words are my reconstruction (e.g. "gince" read as dinte; "zespe/zespi" spelled both ways; examples for k',g' africatization unreadable and not used).
- **SPI-18**: Stela Spînu, "Tendințe fonetice și lexicale în graiurile românești din Republica Moldova", Fonetică și dialectologie XXXVII (2018) 133–140, diacronia.ro A29393. Based on the Atlasul lingvistic moldovenesc (1957–65) and 2015–16 field surveys in 34 localities; repeatedly notes that older features survive mainly among elderly speakers.
- Also seen but not read in full: Spînu 2009 (north-east of the Republic of Moldova, diacronia A25956: acoló, bólnav, dúșman, oftícă; ridică/rîdică; overlaps with Maramureș features), Spînu 2011 book "Graiurile românești din nord-estul Republicii Moldova", Turculeț 2002 monograph on the Câmpulung area (review diacronia R36).

## Conflicts between sources
- **dz**: EN-MOL lists affricate dz in Moldavian (dzîk for zic); SPI-18 says the surveyed Republic of Moldova speech does NOT keep the old dz. No dz rule in either dialect.
- **f**: southern Moldavia f→h (EN-MOL, Căprioară) vs northern Moldavia/Bessarabia f→ș (EN-MOL, SPI-18 "șin"). The app uses h for "Moldovenesc" and ș for "Basarabean". These are regional, not a clean split.
- **m**: palatalized to n in EN-MOL and SPI-18 (nire = mire) but "generally not" in Creangă's Neamț area (CAP-02). m→n only in Bessarabian and Rădăuț.
- **Moldavian page age**: EN-MOL is the 2016 revision.

## Added in round 3 (Crișean, Ardelenesc)
- **EN-CRI**: en.wikipedia.org "Crișana dialect" (full article; flagged by Wikipedia as needing more citations; cites Spînu 2015 *Dialectologia*, Caragiu Marioțeanu 1975 p. 159, Bădescu, Ursan 2008). Includes an IPA field transcription ("Atunci iar a ieșit și s-a uitat...").
- **EN-TRA**: en.wikipedia.org "Transylvanian varieties of Romanian" (shared features of all Transylvanian varieties; cites Weigand 1908, Petrovici 1941, Ursan 2008, Bădescu).
- **NE-TRA**: Maria Cornelia Rus, "Methodological aspects used to realize a dialectal survey in Bârgău area, Bistrița-Năsăud", Paths of Communication in Postmodernity (2020), diacronia.ro A32442; summarizes north-east Transylvanian features after Rusu (Tratat, 1984) and Guia (2014). Only a literature summary, no new fieldwork results.
- Seen, not used: Farcaș, lingv.ro 2023 (Maramureș/Crișana boundary: ĝ→j south of the Tisa; jińere/ĝińere vs ginere), Uritescu et al., diacronia V158 (statistical analysis of the Crișana dialect and Oaș).
## Not found
- No second independent description of Crișean phonetics beyond EN-CRI + Spînu 2015 (via EN-CRI). Treat Crișean rules as single-source. ro.wikipedia pages still unreadable.
- Conflict: EN-TRA says the sibilant vowel shift can also apply after ș, j, r in some varieties; EN-CRI says ș, j sometimes palatalize instead (ușă→ușʲe). Only s, z, ț are implemented.

## Added in round 4 (Muntenesc, Oltenesc, Timocean)
- **EN-WAL**: en.wikipedia.org "Wallachian dialect" (full article; flagged by Wikipedia as needing citations; cites Avram & Sala 2000, Caragiu Marioțeanu 1975 p. 173, Ursan 2008, Bădescu). Includes an IPA field transcription (midwife/ godparent scene) for test cases.
- **OEAW**: Annemarie Sorescu-Marinković, "Timok Romanian (Vlach)", Austrian Academy of Sciences, oeaw.ac.at/vlach (full page): two groups, Ungureni (west, Banat-like) and Țărani (east, western-Oltenian-like); main differences are t, d + e, i and the affricates č, ǧ (Pătruț 1942, Petrovici 1942); also Weigand 1900; Neiescu, Beltechi, Mocanu 2006 atlas.
- **RO-BAN**: search-result snippet (only) of ro.wikipedia "Graiul bănățean": Ungureni forms cinci→ś inś, frunte→frunče, unde→unge, dinte→ghince, and "dies > dzî". Not read in full; treat as unverified wording.
- Also seen: Hutanu & Sorescu-Marinković 2015 (four dialect groups in Eastern Serbia: ungureni, țărani, plus two more; the țărani share much with Muntenian), napocanews 2026 note on the Neiescu atlas (palatalized labials in some contexts), Mărgărit 2021 (Uzdin, Banat dialect literature, diacronia A32495).
## Corrections (important)
- My first-draft "labial palatalization" rule for Muntenian and Oltenian is **removed**: EN-WAL says labials stay unchanged before front vowels in Wallachian, and palatalization appears only in NE/E Muntenia (attributed to recent migrations) and, in a different form (fkʲ, vgʲ), in southern Oltenia.
- Very little differs from standard Romanian in central Muntenian; this is expected because standard pronunciation is largely based on it (EN-WAL).
- Timok data is thin: only the Ungureni/Țărani split and a handful of forms.
